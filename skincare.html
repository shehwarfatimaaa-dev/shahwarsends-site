How a 1-month trial became a retention engine for a skincare brand

My favourite case study to share. We started with a simple one-month trial for a growing skincare and beauty brand, and turned email retention marketing into a core revenue channel.

Industry
DTC skincare and beauty
Scope
Email, flows, subscriptions

1-month trial
The starting point

It began with “let’s test this for one month.”

The goal was never to just send more emails. It was to build an email retention marketing system that turns existing customers into repeat buyers, recovers lost revenue and makes email a predictable revenue channel for the brand.

Very quickly we saw a bigger opportunity. This brand was not only selling skincare products. It had the foundation for a repeat-purchase and subscription-based customer lifecycle.

So instead of treating email as one more promotional channel, we built a system around retention, subscriptions, lifecycle automation and revenue attribution.

The result is a retention engine designed to keep generating revenue long after the first purchase.

The challenge

What happens after someone buys?

Like many DTC skincare and beauty brands, this one was investing heavily in acquiring customers. But acquisition only gets you the first purchase.

A first-time customer can become a repeat customer, a subscriber, a bundle buyer or a long-term VIP. Without the right systems, those opportunities are easy to lose.

For a skincare brand, timing matters enormously. Someone who buys a serum today needs a replenishment reminder weeks later. Someone who buys a cleanser is a natural fit for a moisturizer. A subscriber should never get the same promotions as a one-time buyer.

  • First-time customers were not being fully nurtured
  • Abandoned carts needed stronger recovery
  • Post-purchase emails could do far more than order updates
  • Customers were not being taught what to use next
  • Replenishment windows were being missed
  • Subscribers had no lifecycle of their own
  • One-time buyers and subscribers got identical messaging
  • Campaigns and automated flows were not working together
  • Revenue attribution stopped at vanity metrics

Four tools, one connected customer journey

We used the brand’s existing technology to connect what customers do with what we send them, and what that earns.

Customer behavior→Segmentation→Messaging→Retention→Revenue

The strategy

We rebuilt the journey from subscriber to VIP

Nine moves, each one tied to a stage of the customer lifecycle. This is what ecommerce email marketing for beauty brands looks like when it is built around the customer.

  1. 1Subscriber
  2. 2First purchase
  3. 3Post-purchase education
  4. 4Product usage
  5. 5Replenishment
  6. 6Second purchase
  7. 7Subscription
  8. 8VIP customer
01

Customer journey

We mapped everything that happens from the moment someone joins the list, through the first purchase and beyond. Every flow and campaign after this was built to move customers naturally to the next stage.

02

Welcome series

More than “here’s 10% off.” The welcome journey introduces the brand, builds trust, explains product benefits and shows what makes the formulas different, so a new subscriber becomes a first-time customer who knows what to expect.

03

Abandoned cart recovery

Structured around what actually stops a skincare purchase, so recovery never feels like a wall of “buy now.”

  • Product benefits
  • Objections
  • Trust
  • Social proof
  • Urgency
  • Brand positioning
  • Previous behavior
04

Post-purchase retention

One of the most important areas. The journey should not end at the order confirmation, so we treated post-purchase email as a retention opportunity, not an order-update channel.

  • Usage instructions
  • Product education
  • What results to expect
  • Application tips
  • Complementary products
  • Replenishment reminders
05

Replenishment

Skincare is made for replenishment marketing. Instead of waiting for a customer to think “I’m running out,” we used purchase behavior and product timing to reach them before that point.

06

Subscription retention

A subscription is not automatically a retained customer. Each stage in Recharge got its own communication path, which turned the subscription program from a billing mechanism into part of the retention strategy.

  • Active subscribersProduct value, education and continued engagement.
  • Approaching next orderTimely reminders and relevant product messages.
  • SkippedA skip does not mean the customer is lost.
  • PausedRe-engagement based on what the customer needs.
  • CancelledWin-back emails instead of writing them off.
  • Former subscribersTargeted reactivation campaigns.
07

Segmentation

No more one message for everyone. Audiences were built around behavior and lifecycle stage, which made personalized email marketing for skincare customers possible.

  • New subscribers
  • First-time purchasers
  • Repeat customers
  • High-value customers
  • Active subscribers
  • At-risk customers
  • Lapsed customers
  • Highly engaged
  • Bought specific products
08

Campaign strategy

Campaigns were built around more than discounts, balancing revenue, education, retention and brand building.

  • Skincare routines
  • Ingredient education
  • Product benefits
  • Customer questions
  • Seasonal campaigns
  • New launches
  • Cross-sells
  • Bundles
  • Social proof
  • Customer stories
  • Subscription offers
09

Flows versus campaigns

We tracked how much revenue came from automated flows against one-time campaigns. Flows won, which proves the principle: your email revenue should not depend on how many campaigns you send this week.

What we learned

Acquisition gets the customer in the door. Retention decides what happens next.

For skincare and beauty brands, the real opportunity does not stop when someone clicks “Buy now.” It starts there. A customer who buys a cleanser today can become a customer for the whole routine.

That is why email retention marketing should be a core growth system for a DTC skincare brand, not an afterthought, and why the goal was never to make the brand dependent on constant discounts.
