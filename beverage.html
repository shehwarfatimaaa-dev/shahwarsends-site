The problem

Email wasn’t really doing the work

This brand had an audience it could sell to again. What it didn’t have was a system behind that audience.

So instead of asking “how can we send more emails?” we asked a better question.

How can we turn the customers they already acquired into a predictable retention channel?

The interesting part is that we weren’t optimizing a huge email machine. We were building one.

  • No retention engine behind the brand
  • No real lifecycle strategy
  • No meaningful revenue from automated flows
  • Campaigns weren’t part of a bigger customer journey
  • Customers stopped hearing from the brand after they bought
  • The only lever was another generic “10% off”
Why beverages are built for retention

The product gets consumed

Customers drink it. They run out. They reorder, try another flavor, discover another product and come back.

That means the brand already had a natural repeat-purchase cycle. Our job was to build the email system around it.

Drink it→Run out→Reorder→Try a new flavor→Buy a bundle→Come back
The shift

From sending emails to building a journey

It sounds simple. Building the infrastructure behind each stage is where the work happened.

What we did

Five moves that gave email a reason to exist

01

Built the lifecycle from the ground up

Rather than flooding the list with campaigns, we started with the moments that actually influence customer behavior.

  • WelcomeMore than a discount. An introduction to the brand, the products and the reasons to buy.
  • Abandoned cartRecovering customers who already showed purchase intent.
  • Post-purchaseEducation, expectations and relevant recommendations after checkout.
  • ReplenishmentTimed around when customers are ready for more.
  • Re-engagementCustomers who went quiet weren’t treated as lost.
02

Made flows do the heavy lifting

Automated flows don’t depend on someone manually sending a campaign every time the brand wants another revenue opportunity. They respond to customer behavior, and the system answers each moment.

  • Someone joins
  • Someone browses
  • Someone buys
  • Someone abandons
  • Someone comes back
  • Someone goes quiet
03

Gave campaigns a job beyond “send a promo”

A calendar of sale, sale, sale is not a retention strategy. The goal wasn’t to make the inbox louder. It was to make each send more intentional.

  • Product discovery
  • Education
  • Flavor exploration
  • Seasonal relevance
  • New products
  • Bundles
  • Cross-sells
  • Social proof
  • Customer stories
  • Promotions
04

Started thinking about the second purchase

For a consumable DTC brand, the second purchase changes the relationship. The customer already trusted the brand enough to buy, so the conversation can move on.

  • What did they buy?
  • What might they need next?
  • When might they need it again?
  • What else would genuinely make sense for them?
05

Connected campaigns and flows

One of the biggest mistakes in ecommerce email marketing is treating campaigns and flows as separate things. Campaigns create demand. Flows respond to behavior. Post-purchase continues the relationship, replenishment creates the next order and re-engagement revives a quiet customer. Each piece had a role.
