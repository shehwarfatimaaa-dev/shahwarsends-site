Email Retention for Beverage and Consumable Brands

Short answer

Beverage and consumable brands retain customers by matching email to the consumption cycle: remind people to reorder just before they run out, help first-time buyers find a second flavor, and move repeat buyers to bundles or a subscription. The single most important goal is the second purchase, because that is where a trial turns into a habit.

Email retention for beverage and consumable brands comes down to one idea: your product gets finished, so your emails should follow the rhythm of how fast it gets finished. Remind people before they run out, help them find the next flavor, and make reordering easier each time.

This applies to coffee, tea, drink mixes, sodas, snacks, supplements and anything else people eat or drink and then need again. Here is how I set it up.

Start with the consumption cycle

Everything in a consumable retention program hangs on one number you have to work out yourself: how long a typical order lasts. It depends on pack size, servings per pack, and whether one person or a whole household is drinking it.

I estimate it two ways and compare:

  • On paper. Servings in the pack divided by realistic servings per day.
  • From orders. The typical gap between repeat orders of the same product and pack size.

When the two disagree, I trust the order data. A variety pack sampled slowly and a case of a daily drink behave very differently, even at the same serving count. Work out the cycle per product and pack size, not one figure for the whole store.

Make the second purchase the main goal

A first order of a drink is a taste test. The customer has not decided anything yet. The second order is the real conversion, so I build the whole post-purchase flow around getting it.

That means the emails between delivery and the end of the first pack have a job:

  • Show how to get the best result: how to brew it, mix it, chill it or serve it.
  • Give them more occasions to use it, such as recipes or times of day, so the pack empties sooner and the habit forms.
  • Ask which flavor they liked. A reply or a click is data you can segment on.
  • Put the reorder in front of them before the pack is empty, not after.

The first order is a taste test. The second order is the customer deciding to keep you.

Reorder reminders that arrive on time

After the second purchase, a standing replenishment flow takes over. Klaviyo's replenishment flow guide describes the standard build: trigger on Placed Order, filter to the product, and add a filter so anyone who has bought again since entering the flow drops out. For timing, its example is a 30 day supply with the first reminder around day 25.

The same guide suggests two reminders, then one follow-up after the expected cycle has passed that carries an extra incentive. I like that order because it keeps discounts for people who are actually late, instead of handing them to customers who were going to reorder anyway.

Three things I always check:

  • Delivery time is included. The reminder has to land early enough for the new order to arrive before the old one is gone.
  • Subscribers are excluded. They already have an order coming.
  • Each pack size has its own delay. A sample pack and a bulk case cannot share a timer.

For stores with mixed baskets, Klaviyo also predicts an expected date of next order per customer. Its predictive analytics documentation notes this is not meant to replace a replenishment flow for products with a known cycle, so I use it as a backup signal.

Flavor discovery and bundles

Flavor fatigue is the quiet reason consumable customers drift away. They do not dislike the brand. They are just bored of the one flavor they bought. Discovery emails fix that.

  • Cross-flavor recommendations. Segment by flavor purchased and suggest the closest neighbor, not the bestseller they already have.
  • Variety packs for first-timers. A sampler lowers the risk of picking wrong and tells you what they like.
  • Build-your-own bundles for repeat buyers. Once someone knows their favorites, let them mix a larger box.
  • Bigger packs for heavy users. If someone reorders faster than the cycle predicts, offer the larger size.

Bundles also raise order value without a discount, and a larger order means a longer gap before the next one. Adjust your reminder timing for bundle buyers so you do not nudge them while the cupboard is still full.

When to pitch a subscription

I do not push subscriptions on the first order of a product someone has never tasted. The better moment is after the second or third order of the same item, when the habit is visible in the data.

  • Lead with convenience: never running out, and no need to remember.
  • Be clear about control: skip, swap flavors, change the date, cancel.
  • Suggest a frequency that matches their actual reorder gap, not a default.
  • Once subscribed, send an upcoming order email with the option to swap flavors or add something.

Seasonal campaigns

Drinks are tied to weather and occasions more than most products, which gives you a natural campaign calendar that does not rely on sales.

Limited flavors do double duty. They give lapsed customers a reason to come back and give regulars something new without changing their usual order.

What to do next

Start here
  • Work out the real reorder gap for your top product and pack size
  • Build the first order to second order flow with usage and recipe content
  • Set up a product-specific replenishment flow and exclude subscribers
  • Create segments by flavor purchased for cross-flavor emails
  • Add a subscription pitch for customers with two or more orders of the same item
  • Map four seasonal campaigns for the year ahead

If you only do one of these, do the second-purchase flow. Every other part of the program depends on having customers who came back once.

Questions people ask

When should a beverage brand send a reorder reminder?

A few days before the customer is likely to run out, with enough time for delivery. Base the delay on the real gap between repeat orders for that product and pack size.

What is a replenishment flow?

It is an automated email or SMS sequence triggered by an order that reminds the customer to buy again when the product should be running low. People who reorder are removed from the flow.

Should I offer a discount in reorder emails?

Not in the first reminder. Save any incentive for customers who have passed their usual reorder date without buying.

How do I get first-time beverage buyers to order again?

Help them enjoy the first pack with preparation tips and recipes, ask which flavor they liked, and offer the reorder or a second flavor before the pack is finished.
